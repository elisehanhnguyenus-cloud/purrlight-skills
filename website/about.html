It started with a rainy October evening
and a cat with a tiny suitcase

Purrlight Studio is a small Vietnamese-American family studio in Pearland, Texas — full of yarn, fabric scraps, half-finished baby dresses, and one very particular tuxedo cat named Merlin.

The cat who was waiting for us

Merlin came to us on a rainy October evening at the local shelter — sitting at the very back of his kennel, paws crossed, watching us as if he had been waiting all afternoon. He’d been found with no microchip and no collar. Only a small brown leather suitcase, a hand-stitched blue beret, and a tiny notebook with three pages of sketches in it.

We adopted him that day. He was supposed to be just our cat. But within a month he had reorganized our living room into what looked suspiciously like a small studio — and by the next summer, we were sewing little dolls in the evenings while Merlin inspected every batch like a studio foreman. We asked him: are these yours, or ours? He blinked very slowly. We took that as a partnership agreement.

The cat who takes notes

Merlin sleeps fourteen hours a day and looks, frankly, useless during most of them. And yet we keep finding small sketches on the counter in the morning — tiny watercolors of birds we didn’t see him watch, a diagram of our kitchen, once a drawing of a tree that does not grow in Texas. Every doll and keepsake that leaves our studio begins on the worktable beneath his curious, half-tilted gaze.

Two homes, one pair of hands after another

Our story stretches between two places. Every piece is designed here in Pearland, Texas. And each one is brought to life in small batches by our family of artisan makers in Vietnam — skilled hands that have been crocheting and sewing for decades, paid fairly and never rushed.

We say “made slowly, with love” because it’s literally true: a single doll can take hours of stitching. We think you can feel that when you hold one.

Why “Purrlight”?

The name is a soft pun in our home — “purr” for the soundtrack of our days, and “light” for the small lamp we keep on at the worktable. The one Merlin sits beside while he does whatever it is he does: sketching small flowers from the yard, watching the cars go by, turning pages of a notebook only he can read.

Some evenings, when he sits very still on the windowsill in his little beret and stares up at the moon, we like to imagine he is thinking of somewhere far away. We are glad, for now, that he is staying.

What we believe

Small batches over mass production. Keepsakes over clutter. Materials chosen for small hands and curious paws. And honest craft — when something is made to order, we say so, and when it takes three weeks, that’s because hands are not machines.

— Mama Purr & Papa Purr (and Merlin, who is napping)
